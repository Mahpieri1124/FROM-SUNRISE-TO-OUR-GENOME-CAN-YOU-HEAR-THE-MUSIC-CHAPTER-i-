## 1. Central Question

> **From sunrise to our genome --- can you hear the music?**

This repository develops a research programme around a deliberately open
question:

**How might the physical acoustic environment, natural soundscapes,
human auditory biology, musicality, cognition, physiology, gene
regulation, and cultural evolution be connected --- without assuming
that these connections are already established as one causal chain?**

The project treats "music" at several levels:

1.  physical structure and acoustic phenomena;
2.  natural soundscapes;
3.  auditory perception and prediction;
4.  human musicality;
5.  social coordination and cultural transmission;
6.  neuroplasticity and physiological regulation;
7.  genomic and transcriptomic correlates;
8.  long-term human--environment co-evolution.

The phrase **"Can You Hear the Music?"** is therefore both a scientific
question and a philosophical invitation. It is not a claim that the
Universe literally contains a human-like musical code.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Why This Extension Matters

The foundational Chapter I established an interdisciplinary architecture
linking electrical engineering, complex systems, acoustics, music
cognition, human cognitive systems and artificial intelligence. The
later research trajectory explicitly identifies acoustic and cognitive
priming as an origin point and calls for systematic measurement and
empirical validation.

The present proposal narrows that broad intellectual space into a more
testable research frontier:

> **environmental sound → auditory processing → musicality/cognition →
> physiological and neural response → molecular/transcriptomic response
> → development and adaptation**

The arrow symbols represent candidate relationships to be tested, not
established causal laws.

## 3. What the Existing Literature Already Shows

The scientific literature already provides substantial evidence that:

-   musicality has biological and cognitive components;
-   rhythm, beat and temporal prediction are supported by identifiable
    neural mechanisms;
-   individual differences in musicality have genetic and environmental
    components;
-   large-scale genetic studies have identified genetic correlations
    involving musical rhythm and language-related traits;
-   natural sound exposure can improve some measures of stress recovery
    and physiological restoration;
-   music-making is associated with psychological, physiological and
    social wellbeing;
-   music exposure and musical training can be associated with changes
    in gene expression, neurotrophins and neuroendocrine activity.

However, these literatures remain distributed across different
disciplines.

The 2025 systematic review of music-induced molecular neuroplasticity
identified only 15 qualifying human studies from 3,239 records and
concluded that molecular mechanisms remain insufficiently understood.

The project therefore does **not** claim to have discovered the
existence of a music--genome relationship. Instead, it proposes a more
integrated research architecture for investigating the relationship
across environmental, acoustic, cognitive, neural, molecular and
evolutionary levels.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. The Core Research Gap

The principal gap is **integration plus causal resolution**.

The literature contains:

-   music genetics;
-   musicality and evolution;
-   auditory neuroscience;
-   predictive processing of rhythm;
-   music and wellbeing;
-   natural sound and health;
-   music-induced molecular changes;
-   gene--environment interaction;
-   cultural evolution.

What remains comparatively underdeveloped is a single experimentally
disciplined framework that asks whether these layers can be connected
through measurable intermediate mechanisms.

The project therefore proposes:

> **A multiscale Human Acoustic--Musical--Biological Continuum (HAMBC)
> as a research architecture, not as an established theory.**

The framework should remain open to the possibility that some links are
weak, indirect, non-causal, context-dependent, or absent.

## 5. Primary Research Questions

### RQ1 --- Acoustic environment

Which measurable properties of natural and human-made soundscapes are
associated with restoration, attention, stress regulation and affective
state?

### RQ2 --- Musicality

Which aspects of human musicality appear to be biologically constrained,
developmentally emergent, culturally shaped, or environmentally plastic?

### RQ3 --- Neural mechanisms

How do rhythm, temporal prediction, synchronisation, pitch and acoustic
complexity interact with neural dynamics?

### RQ4 --- Molecular response

Under controlled conditions, does exposure to music or natural sound
produce reproducible changes in peripheral transcriptomic, epigenetic,
neuroendocrine or inflammatory markers?

### RQ5 --- Gene--environment interaction

Do genetic differences associated with musical aptitude modify
physiological, neural or molecular responses to music?

### RQ6 --- Evolution

Which components of musicality are plausibly ancient biological
capacities, and which are better explained as cultural elaborations?

### RQ7 --- Human wellbeing

Can acoustic and musical environments be designed as low-risk,
non-coercive components of nature-based wellbeing and health-oriented
environments?

### RQ8 --- Civilizational relevance

If individual wellbeing, social synchrony and intercultural musical
participation can be improved reliably, could these mechanisms
contribute indirectly to social cohesion and positive peace?

RQ8 is deliberately positioned as a **long-horizon hypothesis**, not a
direct empirical claim.

## 6. Central Hypothesis Family

### H1 --- Acoustic restoration

Selected natural soundscapes will produce measurable differences in
stress and restoration indicators compared with selected urban-noise
conditions.

### H2 --- Musical prediction

Structured rhythmic and temporal stimuli will engage predictive and
synchronisation mechanisms that differ from acoustically matched but
temporally unstructured controls.

### H3 --- Musicality as a multicomponent phenotype

Musicality will be better explained by multiple partially independent
perceptual, cognitive, motor, affective and social components than by a
single latent ability.

### H4 --- Gene--environment interaction

Individual genetic background and musical experience may moderate
responses to musical stimulation.

### H5 --- Molecular response

Some music or natural-sound exposures will produce measurable molecular
responses, but effect sizes, tissue specificity, persistence and causal
relevance will vary substantially.

### H6 --- Experience-dependent plasticity

Repeated active musical engagement will produce stronger or more
persistent neuroplastic signatures than brief passive exposure, subject
to task and population.

### H7 --- Multiscale coupling

The strongest explanatory models will connect acoustic features to
intermediate neural/psychological variables before attempting to infer
molecular or evolutionary consequences.

### H8 --- Wellbeing application

Nature-based environments incorporating carefully designed acoustic
conditions may improve selected wellbeing outcomes without requiring
high-intensity technological intervention.

### H9 --- Social pathway

Participatory, voluntary musical activities may facilitate social
connection under some conditions; this should be tested rather than
assumed.

### H10 --- Positive peace

Any contribution to positive peace is expected to be indirect and
mediated through wellbeing, social trust, equitable participation,
environmental stewardship and institutional conditions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. What This Project Does NOT Claim

It does not claim that:

-   music is literally encoded in the genome;
-   music changes DNA sequence;
-   listening to music permanently rewrites a person's genome;
-   every natural sound is therapeutically beneficial;
-   acoustic music is universally superior to electronic music;
-   genes determine musical ability;
-   music evolved for one single purpose;
-   nature exposure automatically produces peace;
-   tourism automatically produces peace;
-   individual calm automatically produces positive peace;
-   or the project has already demonstrated a causal path from sunrise
    to genome.

These would exceed the current evidence.

## 9. Experimental Strategy

### Phase 1 --- Acoustic laboratory studies

Compare:

-   natural soundscapes;
-   structured acoustic music;
-   electronically reproduced music;
-   urban noise;
-   spectrally matched controls;
-   silence/rest conditions.

Measure:

-   heart rate;
-   heart-rate variability;
-   blood pressure;
-   electrodermal activity;
-   cortisol where appropriate;
-   subjective stress;
-   affect;
-   attention;
-   cognitive performance.

### Phase 2 --- Neurophysiology

Use appropriate methods such as:

-   EEG/MEG where available;
-   pupillometry;
-   eye tracking;
-   behavioural synchronisation tasks;
-   computational modelling of temporal prediction.

### Phase 3 --- Molecular biology

Where ethically and technically justified:

-   peripheral blood transcriptomics;
-   targeted gene-expression panels;
-   microRNA;
-   epigenetic markers;
-   neurotrophins;
-   inflammatory markers;
-   endocrine measures.

The study must distinguish **gene expression** from **DNA sequence**.

### Phase 4 --- Gene--environment interaction

Use sufficiently powered cohorts to investigate whether genetic
variation moderates musical perception, training response or
molecular/physiological response.

Candidate-gene claims should not be treated as established without
replication. Modern analyses should prioritise genome-wide and polygenic
approaches where scientifically appropriate.

### Phase 5 --- Naturalistic field studies

Study:

-   forests;
-   coastlines;
-   mountains;
-   rural environments;
-   urban parks;
-   acoustic retreat environments.

Measure both acoustic ecology and human outcomes.

### Phase 6 --- Social and tourism applications

Only after lower-level validation:

-   nature-based health tourism;
-   acoustic wellbeing retreats;
-   community music;
-   intercultural music programmes;
-   regenerative tourism.

### Phase 7 --- Positive peace

This is the highest-level hypothesis and should not be used as a proxy
for evidence from lower-level experiments.

## 10. Methodological Safeguards

The project adopts the following rules:

1.  **Observation ≠ hypothesis.**
2.  **Association ≠ causation.**
3.  **Gene expression ≠ DNA sequence change.**
4.  **Heritability ≠ genetic determinism.**
5.  **Musical universality ≠ one evolutionary function.**
6.  **Wellbeing ≠ clinical treatment.**
7.  **Individual calm ≠ positive peace.**
8.  **Conceptual continuity ≠ scientific validation.**
9.  **Correlation across levels ≠ causal multiscale mechanism.**
10. **Replication must precede strong mechanistic claims.**

This repository is an extension of the existing *From Sunrise to Our
Genome* research trajectory.

foundational node and later records describe the trajectory from
acoustic/cognitive priming toward physiological, cognitive, human--AI
and civilizational research.
