# Literature Gap Analysis

## From Sunrise to Our Genome --- Can You Hear the Music?

### Global Academic Positioning --- September 2026

## 1. Executive Assessment

The research area is not empty. On the contrary, several component
literatures are mature or rapidly developing.

The originality opportunity is therefore **integrative rather than
foundational**.

The project should not claim:

> "Nobody has studied music, genes, nature or wellbeing."

That would be incorrect.

The defensible claim is:

> **Existing research contains strong but fragmented literatures on
> musicality, genetics, auditory neuroscience, natural sound, wellbeing,
> molecular neuroplasticity and cultural evolution. The proposed project
> seeks to integrate these domains into one empirically falsifiable
> multiscale research programme while explicitly testing, rather than
> assuming, the links between them.**

## 3. Key Literature

### 3.1 Biology and evolution of musicality

Honing and colleagues argue for studying musicality as a biologically
constrained capacity with multiple components rather than treating
"music" as a single evolved adaptation.

A 2026 *Current Biology* synthesis similarly emphasises innate
predispositions in rhythm, pitch and temporal expectation while warning
that different components may have different evolutionary histories.

**Implication:** the project is aligned with the current direction of
the field, not outside it.

### 3.2 Genetics

The genetic basis of music ability has been reviewed for more than a
decade. Earlier work identified candidate loci but suffered from small
samples and phenotype-definition problems.

The 2024/2025 *Nature Human Behaviour* study, with very large samples,
reported shared genetic architecture involving musical rhythm and
language-related traits.

**Implication:** claims about a biological basis for musicality are
increasingly credible, but claims about deterministic "music genes" are
not.

### 3.3 Natural sound

A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis found that natural sound exposure
can reduce anxiety and alter several physiological recovery measures.

**Implication:** the environmental-acoustic branch has empirical
support.

### 3.4 Music and wellbeing

A 2026 systematic review of 50 quantitative studies found positive
psychological, physiological and social wellbeing outcomes from
music-making, while also noting substantial methodological heterogeneity
and an overrepresentation of group singing.

**Implication:** wellbeing is promising but requires better phenotype
and intervention standardisation.

### 3.5 Molecular biology

The 2015 transcriptome study by Kanduri et al. demonstrated differential
gene expression in peripheral blood following music listening in
particular participant groups.

The 2025 systematic review of music-induced molecular neuroplasticity
synthesised 15 human studies and concluded that gene expression,
neurotrophins and neuroendocrine processes can be modulated by
music-based interventions and training, while molecular mechanisms
remain insufficiently understood.

**Implication:** the "music ↔ molecular biology" bridge exists, but it
is far from complete.

## 4. The Most Important Conceptual Gap

The major opportunity is not a single missing fact.

It is the absence of a sufficiently disciplined **multiscale causal
architecture**.

Most studies stop at one or two levels:

-   acoustic → psychological;
-   music → neural;
-   music → wellbeing;
-   genotype → musicality;
-   music → transcriptomics;
-   music-making → social bonding.

The proposed project asks whether these can be nested without committing
the common error of treating correlation between adjacent levels as
proof of a causal chain across all levels.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Five Specific Research Gaps

### Gap A --- Environmental sound versus music

Natural sound exposure and musical stimulation are usually studied
separately.

A controlled factorial design could test:

**naturalness × musical structure × predictability × acoustic
intensity**

and determine whether "nature" and "music" share or diverge in their
mechanisms.

### Gap B --- Acoustic features versus musical labels

Many studies classify stimuli as "music" or "noise."

A more rigorous approach would quantify acoustic features directly.

This enables models based on:

-   predictability;
-   temporal regularity;
-   spectral complexity;
-   roughness;
-   entropy;
-   amplitude modulation;
-   harmonicity;
-   spatial variation.

### Gap C --- Molecular response

The molecular literature is small and heterogeneous.

Future studies need:

-   larger samples;
-   preregistration;
-   repeated sampling;
-   multiple control conditions;
-   correction for cell-type composition;
-   replication;
-   independent cohorts.

### Gap D --- Gene--environment interaction

A major unanswered question is whether genetic differences associated
with musical aptitude change the physiological or molecular response to
the same acoustic stimulus.

This requires much larger samples than many early music-genetics
studies.

### Gap E --- Evolutionary continuity

The field has evidence from humans, infants and some non-human
vocal-learning species.

However, evolutionary narratives remain difficult to establish.

The project should therefore distinguish:

1.  evolutionary ancestry;
2.  biological constraint;
3.  developmental plasticity;
4.  cultural evolution;
5.  cultural invention.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. What Would Count as a Strong Contribution?

A strong paper from this programme would not merely state that music is
connected to genes.

It would demonstrate something like:

> A preregistered, replicated relationship between a defined acoustic
> feature, a defined perceptual/neural mechanism, a defined
> physiological mediator, and a reproducible molecular signature, while
> controlling for expectation, musical experience, demographic factors
> and relevant genetic structure.

That would constitute a substantial scientific contribution.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. What Would NOT Count as a Strong Contribution?

The following should not be treated as sufficient:

-   a poetic analogy between sunrise and DNA;
-   a single candidate-gene association;
-   a small uncontrolled music experiment;
-   a post-hoc transcriptomic result without replication;
-   a correlation between musicality and wellbeing;
-   a claim that all natural sound is therapeutic;
-   a claim that music is "encoded in the genome";
-   a philosophical claim presented as molecular biology.

**Currently low to moderate**

**Potentially high after narrowing to a clearly testable empirical

## 9. Recommended Scientific Position

The strongest formulation is:

> **The project proposes a multiscale research architecture linking
> environmental acoustics, musicality, neural prediction, physiological
> regulation and molecular biology, with the explicit aim of identifying
> which links are empirically supported, which are context-dependent,
> and which should be rejected.**

That formulation is compatible with the literature and preserves the
originality of the integrative question without overstating novelty.

## 11. Final Gap Statement

The project should define its central gap as:

> **There is no established, experimentally validated framework that
> integrates environmental acoustic structure, human musicality, neural
> prediction, physiological regulation and molecular response across
> scales while explicitly separating biological predisposition,
> environmental exposure, cultural learning and causal inference.**

This is the research gap worth testing.
