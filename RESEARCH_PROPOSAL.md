## Abstract

This proposal develops an empirical research programme originating from
the interdisciplinary question "From Sunrise to Our Genome --- Can You
Hear the Music?"

The project investigates whether measurable relationships can be
established across environmental acoustics, natural soundscapes,
auditory perception, musicality, cognition, neural dynamics,
physiological regulation, gene expression and gene--environment
interaction.

The central scientific problem is not whether "music is in the genome."
That formulation is currently unsupported and biologically ambiguous.
The scientifically tractable problem is whether the capacity for
musicality has partly biological and evolutionary foundations, how
environmental exposure and learning interact with those foundations, and
whether controlled musical or natural-sound exposure can produce
reproducible physiological, neural and molecular responses.

Existing research already demonstrates biological and cognitive
components of musicality, genetic contributions to individual
differences, shared genetic architecture between musical rhythm and
language-related traits, natural-sound effects on stress recovery, and
music-associated molecular/neuroplastic changes. Yet the literatures
remain fragmented across music cognition, evolutionary biology, auditory
neuroscience, genetics, genomics, psychophysiology, music medicine and
environmental psychology.

The proposed contribution is therefore an integrative, experimentally
falsifiable architecture rather than a claim of a newly discovered
biological law.

## 1. Background

The foundational Chapter I of *From Sunrise to Our Genome* established
an interdisciplinary research identity spanning acoustics, electrical
engineering, complex systems, music cognition, human cognitive systems
and artificial intelligence. Later research records explicitly describe
the intellectual trajectory as beginning with acoustic and cognitive
priming and expanding toward physiological resilience, human cognition,
human--AI co-evolution and conditions associated with positive peace.

The present proposal deliberately returns to the acoustic/biological
origin point and makes it more empirically precise.

The key question becomes:

> **Can the links between environmental sound, human musicality, neural
> prediction, physiological regulation and molecular biology be measured
> as a multiscale system?**

## 2. Literature Position

### 2.1 Evolution and biology of musicality

Current scholarship increasingly treats musicality as a biologically
constrained capacity rather than music as merely a cultural product. A
2026 *Current Biology* essay synthesises evidence from infants,
cross-cultural research, neuroscience and comparative biology, arguing
for innate predispositions in rhythm, pitch and temporal expectation
while emphasising that components of musicality have distinct
evolutionary histories.

Earlier work has likewise argued for a multicomponent approach because
the evolutionary history of "music" as a whole is difficult to
reconstruct.

### 2.2 Genetics of musical ability

Reviews show that both genetic and environmental factors contribute to
musical ability. Earlier candidate-gene and linkage studies generated
promising signals but were constrained by sample size and phenotype
definition.

The field has since moved toward larger genomic approaches.

A major 2024/2025 *Nature Human Behaviour* study found significant
genetic correlations between musical rhythm traits and language-related
phenotypes and identified shared genetic signals. This is important
because it moves the field beyond simple candidate-gene narratives.

The implication for this proposal is methodological:

> musicality should be treated as a complex, polygenic, developmentally
> and environmentally influenced phenotype.

### 2.3 Neural dynamics and rhythm

A 2024 *Nature Reviews Psychology* review describes rhythm, beat and
metre as fertile domains for linking behavioural, neural, developmental,
genetic and evolutionary research. Predictive-coding and oscillator
models provide testable mechanisms for temporal expectation and
synchronisation.

A 2025 *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* perspective further reviews neural
synchronisation with musical stimuli and its role in expectation.

These literatures provide the mechanistic middle layer required between
acoustic exposure and higher-level claims.

### 2.4 Music and molecular biology

This is one of the most important developments for the present proposal.

Kanduri et al. reported changes in peripheral blood transcriptomic
profiles following a 20-minute exposure to classical music, with
responses differing according to musical experience/aptitude.

The study does **not** demonstrate that music changes DNA sequence. It
demonstrates that environmental exposure can be associated with
measurable changes in gene expression in peripheral blood under a
particular experimental design.

A 2025 systematic review of music-induced molecular neuroplasticity
synthesised 15 human studies from an initial pool of 3,239 records. It
concluded that music-based interventions and musical training have been
associated with gene-expression changes, neurotrophic factors and
neuroendocrine changes, while emphasising that molecular mechanisms
remain insufficiently understood.

This review is simultaneously a foundation and a warning: the field
exists, but its evidence base is still small and heterogeneous.

### 2.5 Natural sound and physiological recovery

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of natural sound exposure
included 15 studies and 1,285 participants. It reported benefits
involving anxiety and several physiological measures including heart
rate, blood pressure and respiratory rate, with possible cognitive
benefits.

This provides a direct bridge between environmental acoustics and
physiological regulation.

However, natural sound exposure is not equivalent to music. The proposal
therefore keeps natural soundscape, acoustic music and artificial/urban
noise as distinct experimental classes.

## 3. Gap Analysis

The principal gap is not that researchers have never studied music and
biology.

They have.

The gap is the absence of a sufficiently integrated and causally
disciplined programme connecting:

**environmental acoustic structure → auditory computation → musicality →
neural synchronisation/prediction → autonomic/endocrine regulation →
molecular response → development and gene--environment interaction.**

A second gap concerns **ecological validity**. Much music research uses
controlled musical stimuli, while environmental-health research often
studies natural sounds. A systematic comparison between natural
soundscapes, acoustic music, electronically reproduced music, and urban
noise remains comparatively underdeveloped.

A third gap concerns **phenotype precision**. "Musicality" is not one
variable. It should be decomposed into rhythm perception, beat
synchronisation, pitch processing, temporal prediction, production,
affective response, social synchrony and related dimensions.

A fourth gap concerns **genomic interpretation**. Existing studies can
show transcriptomic or molecular associations without establishing that
the genome itself is being "rewritten." Future research must
distinguish:

-   DNA sequence;
-   genetic variation;
-   gene expression;
-   epigenetic regulation;
-   RNA/miRNA;
-   proteins;
-   metabolites;
-   endocrine signalling;
-   neural plasticity.

A fifth gap concerns **causal direction**. People with greater musical
aptitude may respond differently to music; that does not mean music
caused their aptitude. Longitudinal and genetically informed designs are
therefore required.

## 4. Research Questions

1.  Which acoustic features of natural environments are associated with
    restoration?
2.  Which features of musical rhythm engage temporal prediction and
    synchronisation?
3.  Which components of musicality show the strongest biological
    constraints?
4.  How do musical experience and genetic variation interact?
5.  Does controlled music exposure produce reproducible molecular
    responses?
6.  Are these responses short-lived, repeated, cumulative or
    context-dependent?
7.  Are natural sounds and music mediated by overlapping or distinct
    mechanisms?
8.  Can acoustic environments be designed for wellbeing without
    overstimulation or coercion?
9.  Can participatory music improve social synchrony or interpersonal
    trust?
10. Under what conditions, if any, could these pathways contribute to
    positive peace?

## 5. Research Design

### Work Package 1 --- Acoustic Characterisation

Build a reproducible acoustic taxonomy using measurable features such
as:

-   spectral distribution;
-   temporal modulation;
-   amplitude variability;
-   roughness;
-   rhythm;
-   predictability;
-   entropy;
-   spatial distribution;
-   naturalness;
-   perceived pleasantness;
-   acoustic diversity.

### Work Package 2 --- Psychophysiology

Randomised controlled exposure studies comparing:

1.  natural sound;
2.  acoustic music;
3.  electronically reproduced music;
4.  urban noise;
5.  silence/rest;
6.  spectrally matched control stimuli.

Primary outcomes may include HRV, heart rate, blood pressure,
electrodermal activity, salivary cortisol where appropriate, subjective
stress and affect.

### Work Package 3 --- Cognitive and Neural Measures

Measure:

-   beat synchronisation;
-   temporal prediction;
-   attention;
-   working memory where justified;
-   EEG/MEG measures;
-   neural entrainment;
-   pupil responses;
-   computational estimates of prediction error.

### Work Package 4 --- Molecular Measures

Where justified and ethically approved:

-   transcriptomics;
-   targeted gene expression;
-   miRNA;
-   epigenetic markers;
-   neurotrophic factors;
-   inflammatory markers;
-   endocrine measures.

### Work Package 5 --- Genetics

Where sample size permits:

-   genome-wide association analyses;
-   polygenic scores;
-   gene--environment interaction;
-   genetic correlation;
-   heritability estimation.

Candidate-gene findings should be treated as exploratory unless
independently replicated.

### Work Package 6 --- Longitudinal Musical Engagement

Compare short exposure with repeated active musical engagement.

The principal question is whether durable changes require:

-   repetition;
-   active participation;
-   skill acquisition;
-   social interaction;
-   or some combination.

### Work Package 7 --- Naturalistic Field Research

Study real environments and measure both:

-   objective acoustic properties;
-   subjective experience;
-   physiological response;
-   behavioural and social outcomes.

### Work Package 8 --- Translational Applications

Only after validation:

-   nature-based health tourism;
-   acoustic wellness environments;
-   educational settings;
-   community music;
-   regenerative tourism.

### Work Package 9 --- Positive Peace

Treat positive peace as a distal outcome requiring separate
social-science research rather than assuming it follows directly from
physiological wellbeing.

## 6. Proposed Causal Model

The project proposes a testable hierarchy:

### Level 1 --- Environment

Nature / built environment / soundscape.

### Level 2 --- Acoustic stimulus

Physical acoustic parameters.

### Level 3 --- Sensory processing

Auditory transduction and perceptual organisation.

### Level 4 --- Cognitive dynamics

Prediction, attention, synchronisation, memory and affect.

### Level 5 --- Neural dynamics

Oscillatory coupling, neural entrainment and network-level processing.

### Level 6 --- Physiological regulation

Autonomic, endocrine and stress-related responses.

### Level 7 --- Molecular response

Gene expression, epigenetic regulation, neurotrophins, inflammatory
pathways and related biomarkers.

### Level 8 --- Development

Repeated exposure, learning, plasticity and individual trajectories.

### Level 9 --- Social/cultural level

Coordination, participation, bonding, cultural transmission.

### Level 10 --- Civilizational level

Potential contribution to wellbeing, social cohesion, sustainable
development and positive peace.

The project does not assume that every level is causally connected.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Falsification Criteria

The programme should be considered weakened or rejected if:

-   natural sound effects disappear under adequately controlled
    replication;
-   music-related molecular effects fail replication;
-   observed effects are fully explained by expectation/placebo/context;
-   genetic associations fail independent replication;
-   supposed causal links are shown to be entirely confounded;
-   social music interventions fail to outperform credible controls;
-   ecological implementations produce net environmental or social harm.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Ethical Framework

Human research must follow appropriate institutional review, informed
consent, privacy and biological-sample governance.

Genomic data require heightened protection.

No participant should be classified as genetically "musical" or
"non-musical" in a deterministic way.

The project must avoid:

-   genetic essentialism;
-   cultural hierarchy;
-   coercive musical intervention;
-   medical claims without clinical evidence;
-   commercial exploitation of sensitive genomic data.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Expected Scientific Contribution

The expected contribution is not a new "music gene."

It is a multiscale research architecture capable of connecting
previously separated literatures and generating experiments that can
determine which connections survive empirical testing.

Potential outputs include:

-   an acoustic phenotype framework;
-   a musicality phenotype framework;
-   a reproducible acoustic exposure protocol;
-   a music--molecular response dataset;
-   a natural-sound vs music comparison;
-   gene--environment analyses;
-   computational models of acoustic prediction;
-   evidence-based acoustic wellbeing environments.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Relation to Positive Peace

Positive peace is deliberately placed at the end of the research
hierarchy.

The proposed logic is:

**healthy environment → restorative sensory experience → improved
wellbeing → greater individual adaptive capacity → possible social
synchrony/cooperation → possible contribution to positive peace.**

This is a hypothesis chain, not a demonstrated causal law.

Any peace claim requires independent measures of social trust,
inclusion, inequality, cooperation, conflict and institutional
conditions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Project Governance Principle

A central methodological rule inherited from the broader research
programme is:

> **Do not let the beauty of an interdisciplinary hypothesis substitute
> for empirical validation.**

A second rule is:

> **Operationalise before expanding.**

The project should prioritise a small number of high-quality experiments
rather than continuously adding new conceptual constructs.

## 13. Final Position

The project should be presented as:

> **A hypothesis-generating interdisciplinary research programme
> investigating whether environmental acoustic structure, human
> musicality, neural prediction, physiological regulation and molecular
> biology can be connected through experimentally measurable
> mechanisms.**

It should not yet be presented as proof that:

> "the genome contains music"

or that:

> "music inevitably creates peace."

The scientific value lies precisely in making these beautiful intuitions
falsifiable.
